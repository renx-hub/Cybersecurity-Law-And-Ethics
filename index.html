Cybersecurity —
Laws and Ethics

 

Unauthorized access to a computer system is a crime the moment it happens — regardless of intent, skill, or whether any damage occurs.

The key question

Do you have permission?

Five ways the law responds

How the law holds hackers accountable

1

Access alone is the crime

Unauthorized access is a punishable offense on its own — no theft or damage required. Penalties scale with intent: basic access carries lighter sentences, while access paired with fraud, system damage, or harm to critical infrastructure can mean years, even life, in prison.

  • Unauthorized access is punishable on its own
  • No theft or damage required
  • Penalties scale with intent
  • Can mean years, even life, in prison
2

Civil liability

Beyond criminal charges, victims can separately sue for damages — covering financial losses, remediation costs, and harm caused. This liability exists independently of any criminal case and can add up to far more than a fine.

  • Victims can separately sue for damages
  • Covers financial losses and remediation costs
  • Applies independently of criminal charges
  • Can exceed any criminal fine
3

Data & system interference RA 10175

Damaging, altering, or disrupting computer data or systems — including denial-of-service attacks — carries the same penalty range as illegal access. But if the target is critical infrastructure (banks, utilities, government systems), the sentence jumps to 12–20 years and fines starting at ₱500,000.

  • Covers damaging, altering, or disrupting data and systems
  • Includes denial-of-service attacks
  • Same penalty range as illegal access
  • Critical infrastructure targets: 12–20 years + ₱500,000 fine
4

Data privacy violations RA 10173

When a hack exposes personal data, a separate law applies. Unauthorized processing or access to personal information carries 1–6 years imprisonment and fines up to ₱4,000,000 — increasing further if the victim is a minor or the data is sensitive.

  • Applies when a hack exposes personal data
  • 1–6 years imprisonment
  • Fines up to ₱4,000,000
  • Harsher if the victim is a minor or the data is sensitive
5

The consequences don't end at sentencing

Beyond prison, a conviction can mean years paying back victims in restitution, losing the very computer access your skills depend on, being denied entry into other countries, facing fresh lawsuits long after release — and a criminal record that follows you, and your family, everywhere you go. One bad decision, a lifetime of paying for it.

  • Years paying restitution to victims
  • Loss of computer and internet access
  • Denied entry into other countries
  • Fresh lawsuits even after release
